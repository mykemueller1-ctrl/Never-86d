## BBQ Mastery

### Brisket
- Trim fat cap to 1/4 inch, remove hard fat
- Rub: coarse black pepper + kosher salt (50/50 "Dalmatian rub") or custom blend
- Smoke at 225-250°F with post oak, hickory, or mesquite
- Wrap at 165-170°F internal (butcher paper for bark, foil for speed/moisture)
- Cook to probe tender (usually 200-205°F internal)
- Rest minimum 1 hour (up to 4 hours in cooler wrapped in towels)
- Slice against the grain, pencil-width for flat, thicker for point
- The "stall" happens around 150-170°F - moisture evaporation cools meat

### Ribs (3-2-1 Method for spare ribs, 2-2-1 for baby backs)
- Remove membrane from bone side
- Apply mustard binder + rub (paprika, brown sugar, garlic, onion, cayenne)
- 3 hours smoke at 225°F unwrapped (bone side down)
- 2 hours wrapped in foil with butter, brown sugar, honey
- 1 hour unwrapped, sauce applied, glaze set
- Done when meat pulls back 1/4 inch from bone ends
- Bend test: ribs crack but don't break when bent

### Pork Butt (Pulled Pork)
- 8-10 lb bone-in Boston butt
- Rub: paprika, brown sugar, garlic, cumin, chili powder, salt, pepper
- Smoke at 225-250°F for 12-16 hours
- Wrap at 160-170°F (optional - speeds cook)
- Pull at 200-205°F internal when probe slides in like butter
- Rest 30-60 min, pull by hand (not shredded with forks for better texture)
- Bark pieces mixed throughout

### Wings (Competition Style)
- Pat completely dry, toss in baking powder + salt (1 tbsp BP per 4 tbsp rub)
- Smoke at 225°F for 30 min to absorb smoke
- Raise to 350-375°F for 30-45 min until skin crisps
- Internal temp 175-180°F for rendered skin
- Sauce: toss immediately after pulling, serve within 2 min
- Alternative: smoke then flash fry 2 min at 375°F for ultimate crisp

## Fryer Mastery
- Standard temp range: 325-375°F, 350°F all-purpose default
- Never let oil drop below 325°F when loading food
- French fries: blanch at 325°F, finish at 375°F (double-fry method)
- Chicken: 350°F for bone-in (12-15 min), 375°F for boneless (3-5 min)
- Fish: 350-375°F (3-5 min depending on thickness)
- Onion rings: 375°F (2-3 min until golden)
- Oil filtration: minimum once daily, twice during heavy volume
- Oil life indicators: dark color, foam, off smell, smoke point drops
- Never mix old and new oil types
- Cool zone at bottom catches debris, extends oil life

## Grill/Charbroiler Mastery
- No thermostat on commercial charbroilers - runs at constant temp
- Preheat 15-20 min before service
- Zone cooking: hottest center, cooler edges
- Steak temps: Rare 120-125°F, MR 130-135°F, Med 135-145°F, MW 145-155°F, Well 155°F+
- Always rest steaks 5 min after pulling (carryover cooking adds 5°F)
- Cross-hatch marks: 45° angle, rotate 90° halfway through each side
- Clean grates between every protein change (wire brush)
- Flare-up control: move food to cool zone, never spray water

## Pizza Excellence
- Dough hydration: 60-65% for deck oven (higher = more open crumb but harder to handle)
- Flour: high-gluten bread flour (12-14% protein)
- Cold ferment 24-72 hours for flavor development
- Deck oven temp: 500-550°F (stone/deck preheated minimum 1 hour)
- Stretch by hand, never roll (preserves gas bubbles)
- Sauce: thin layer, leave 1/2 inch border
- Cheese: low-moisture mozzarella for browning, fresh mozz for Neapolitan
- Bake time: 6-8 min at 500°F, rotate halfway
- Signs of done: crust golden, cheese bubbling with brown spots, bottom sounds hollow

## Hospitality Training

### Table Approach (Steps of Service)
1. Greet within 60 seconds of seating
2. Make eye contact with everyone at the table
3. Introduce yourself by name (at the END of greeting, not beginning)
4. Read the table: business dinner vs. celebration vs. casual
5. Offer water and mention specials/features
6. Suggest a specific drink ("Can I start you with our house margarita?")
7. Return within 2 minutes with drinks, take food order
8. 2-bite check-back after food arrives
9. Pre-bus throughout meal
10. Drop check when appropriate (don't rush, don't ignore)

### Upselling Scripts
- "Would you like to add our loaded fries to that?" (specific add-on)
- "Our Iowa Chop is incredible tonight, it's been smoking since this morning" (feature item)
- "Can I get you a top-shelf pour on that? Tito's instead of well?" (upgrade)
- "Save room for our skillet cookie, it's perfect for sharing" (dessert plant)
- "Another round while you look at the menu?" (drink refill)

### Reading the Room
- Business dinner: formal, efficient, don't interrupt, refill without asking
- Date night: attentive but invisible, romantic pacing, suggest shareable plates
- Families: fast drinks for kids, patient with orders, offer crayons/activities
- Regulars: remember their usual, acknowledge them by name, make them feel VIP
- Large parties: assign a leader, confirm split check early, pace courses

### Complaint Handling (LEARN Method)
- L - Listen without interrupting
- E - Empathize ("I completely understand your frustration")
- A - Apologize sincerely ("I'm sorry this happened")
- R - React/Resolve (fix it NOW - remake, comp, manager visit)
- N - Notify manager (always escalate, even if resolved)

### Split Check Best Practices (PDQ POS)
- Ask at greeting: "Will this be together or separate?"
- On PDQ: open multiple checks per table from the start
- Seat numbers: assign items to seats as you ring them in
- Split by item: move individual items between checks
- Split evenly: divide total equally across X checks
- Never split after payment has started
- Gift cards can be applied to individual split checks

## Beer/Keg Knowledge

### Draft System
- Keg types: 1/2 barrel (15.5 gal/124 pints), 1/4 barrel (7.75 gal/62 pints), 1/6 barrel (5.16 gal/41 pints)
- CO2 pressure: 12-14 PSI for most ales, 14-16 PSI for lagers
- Line temp: 36-38°F from keg to faucet
- Clean lines every 2 weeks minimum (acid + caustic cycle)
- Foam issues: too warm (raise pressure), too cold (lower pressure), dirty lines, kinked lines
- Proper pour: 45° angle, straighten at 2/3 full, 1 inch head

### Common Draft Beers (Iowa bar typical)
- Bud Light, Coors Light, Miller Lite (domestic light lagers)
- Busch Light (Iowa staple)
- Blue Moon (wheat)
- Local/craft rotators
- Guinness (nitrogen tap - different faucet, restrictor plate)
